Pranayama

Three breaths to know

Seated, spine tall, on an empty stomach. Press play to hear the rhythm of each one before you try it.

Kapalbhati

Skull-shining breath

Short, forceful exhales through both nostrils with passive inhales. A cleansing, energising practice that wakes up the abdomen.

  1. Sit tall, spine long, hands resting on the knees.
  2. Exhale sharply by pulling the belly in; let the inhale happen passively.
  3. Start with 20 pumps, rest, then another 20. Build slowly to 50.
3 rounds · morning

Bhramari

Humming-bee breath

Hum like a bee on the exhale with the ears gently closed. Instantly soothing for anxiety, restlessness and headaches.

  1. Close the ears with the thumbs and the eyes with the fingers.
  2. Inhale through the nose, then hum one long “mmmm” on the exhale.
  3. Repeat seven times, letting the sound fill the whole head.
7 rounds · any time

Bhastrika

Bellows breath

Equal, forceful inhales and exhales through the nose — like pumping air into a fire. Warming and activating; use with care.

  1. Sit comfortably with the back straight and shoulders relaxed.
  2. Breathe forcefully in and out through the nose, about one cycle per second.
  3. Do 10–20 breaths, then rest and breathe normally before the next round.
3 rounds · build slowly

Take care. Skip forceful practices (kapalbhati, bhastrika) during pregnancy, or with high blood pressure, heart conditions, hernia, epilepsy or recent surgery. Stop immediately if you feel dizzy, and return to normal breathing. This is education, not medical advice.

The method

Three minutes, every day

Small and repeatable beats heroic and rare. Here is the whole practice.

01

Settle

Sit comfortably. Let your shoulders drop. Nothing to fix, nowhere to be.

02

Follow the light

Inhale as the orb grows, exhale as it returns. Your breath finds its pace.

03

Return

Open your eyes slowly. Carry a fraction of that stillness into the hour.
